6How this was measured

What is measured, what is modelled, and what is invented — stated so the numbers above can be used at the right confidence.

Positions are measured

The camera pans continuously, so a single fixed homography is worthless — it exceeds a 2 m error budget within about a second. Instead the pitch model is registered to one reference frame by optimising camera pose against the detected line markings, and every other frame is tied to that reference by a homography estimated from SIFT correspondences. All 807 frames registered, none failed. Residual position noise is {{NOISE}} m, which is small enough for zones, thirds, shape and occupancy.

Identities are synthetic

Read this before quoting a player number

H01–H11 and A01–A11 are labels for tracks, not people. Real shirt numbers are unreadable at 768 × 432 — a human cannot read them from these frames at 2.4× magnification either, and jersey-number recognition is the acknowledged weakest component of the state-of-the-art SoccerNet pipeline even at full broadcast resolution. Numbers here are assigned by average position from each side's own goal outward, so they carry positional meaning and nothing else. Bands (DEEP / MID / HIGH) are positional, not detected roles — there is deliberately no "GK", because both goalkeepers are off camera for most of this passage.

Distance is the weakest number here

Confidence: low on distance, good on speed

Summing frame-to-frame displacement turns measurement noise into distance. With {{NOISE}} m of residual jitter at 25 fps, unfiltered summation fabricates over 1 000 m/min of pure noise — more than the real signal. A constant-velocity Kalman filter with RTS smoothing brings peak speeds back into the physically possible range (nothing above 11 m/s, max {{TOPSPEED}}), and speeds and sprint counts should be treated as sound. Absolute distance totals should be treated as an upper bound, and compared between players rather than read as true metres.

The ceiling this cannot pass

One camera sees {{ONCAM}} players on average, not 22. That is not a defect of this pipeline — published work on broadcast footage reports 10–16 of 22 visible in a typical frame, and StatsBomb measured an average of 14.35 of 20 outfield players. Any metric that needs all 22 simultaneously — pitch control, off-ball space occupation, true team centroid — cannot be computed honestly from this angle, by anyone. Possession here is a nearest-player proxy gated at 3 m, not event-detected possession, and {{POSS_FRAMES}} frames carried an attribution.
